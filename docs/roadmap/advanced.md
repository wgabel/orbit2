#### Phase 7.1 — Envelope generators and rhythmic triggers (partial: 7.1.1–7.1.5 implemented)

- **7.1.1 — Oscillator-driven AD envelope (implemented):** reusable sample-rate edge detector, continuous oscillator trigger input, shared AD segment state machine, configurable durations/curves, unipolar range prediction, shared parameter UI, five presets, editable impact patch and compiled DSP timing/continuity tests. No dedicated trigger source required. Implementation: [PR #7](https://github.com/wgabel/orbit2/pull/7).
- **7.1.2 — Typed rhythmic ports and adapters (implemented):** Trigger, Gate and Clock contracts, explicit continuous-signal edge/gate adapters, single-source unity event links, nested rack/flattening/validation/persistence support and tokenized event sockets; preserve the existing oscillator trigger route with conditional source selection. Implementation: [PR #8](https://github.com/wgabel/orbit2/pull/8).
- **7.1.3 — Rhythmic sources (implemented — PR #10):** manual trigger and gate runtime actions, free/BPM clock using shared transport, gate duration and trigger fan-out; no graph revision or recompilation on events; generation-safe worklet delivery and timing tests. Implementation: [PR #10](https://github.com/wgabel/orbit2/pull/10).
- **7.1.4 — AR, ADSR and Loop (implemented — PR #12):** gate-aware stage transitions, sustain/release, repeat/rest and rapid retrigger continuity using the same envelope node and conditional parameters; compatible AD migration, initial presets and multi-rate DSP tests. Implementation: [PR #12](https://github.com/wgabel/orbit2/pull/12).
- **7.1.5 — Envelope visualization (implemented):** configured curve, live measured output and DSP stage/progress playhead, compact/expanded shared visualization and portable view preferences; no graph mutation or DSP dependency on display. Implementation: [PR #14](https://github.com/wgabel/orbit2/pull/14).
- **7.1.6 — Presets and worked patches (partial — AD presets and impact composite delivered in 7.1.1, initial AR/ADSR/Loop presets in 7.1.4):** grouped AD/percussion, AR/swell, ADSR/voice, rhythmic modulation presets and independently routed multi-envelope impact examples, validated in CI.
- **7.1.7 — Custom multi-point envelopes (planned):** bounded versioned breakpoint definitions, reusable segment evaluation, atomic edit/history and compatible persistence; documented loop and trigger behavior.

Deliverable: an oscillator can repeatedly trigger a percussion envelope without any new timing node; later explicit trigger, gate and clock nodes can drive several independently shaped envelopes or other event consumers in sample-aligned fashion. Phase 6.18 visualization remains the shared display foundation; sequencers and sample playback reuse the event contracts.

- **7.2.4 — Resonator processor (implemented):** reusable normalized two-state damped-mode Faust emitter, independently modulatable frequency, semitone pitch, T60, excitation/output gain and wet mix, common bypass/visualization, five presets and compatible persistence. Implementation: [PR #34](https://github.com/wgabel/orbit2/pull/34).

Profile CPU/memory, voice stealing and independent releases before increasing voice counts.
- **7.2.7 — Further physical models (planned; Faust-first audit and offline experiments):** reuse verified, license-cleared Faust physical modelling functions where suitable; distinguish existing synthetic approximations from acoustic models. See [detailed increments below](#phase-727--faust-first-physical-models-planned) and [realistic instrument emulation](../instruments/realistic-instrument-emulation.md).
- **7.2.8 — Keyboard performance and continuous pitch sequencing (partial):** shared laptop/on-screen input capture, live latched transposition, editable bounded note patterns, BPM/free seconds step timing including multi-bar ambient durations, sample-aligned looping, glide, quantized live recording, step recording with rests/holds/auto-advance, manual grid editing and atomic pattern history. Deliverable: play and edit a synthesized instrument as an ordinary Orbit2 graph; excite a handpan-like modal bank with short bursts and different pitches/velocities; use the same pitch, gate, envelope and resonator components for sustained and percussive sounds without hardcoded instrument-specific DSP; perform and loop quantized pitch progressions over rhythmic or multi-minute ambient timescales without interrupting continuous drones. ##### Phase 7.2.7 — Faust-first physical models (planned)

User feedback: existing instrument timbres lack realism. Prioritize the [Faust-first specification](../instruments/realistic-instrument-emulation.md) and reuse its models/building blocks when suitable, rather than constructing every real-instrument voice from static waveforms. Preserve existing preset IDs and synthetic alternatives. The substeps are independent where noted; use the 7.3 benchmark baseline before costly DSP expansion.

- **7.2.7a — Dependency and licensing audit (partial; audit merged, [PR #79](https://github.com/wgabel/orbit2/pull/79)):** [Pinned artifact and two candidate audit](../instruments/faust-dependency-audit.md): exact pinned browser bundle identified; Faust 2.90.0, libraries 2.74.2, physmodels.lib 1.2.1 includes LGPL exception; minimal pluck and flute compile with a recorded dependency inventory. Local research may proceed; full source/release obligations, notices and final wrapper dependency review remain a production gate. Other candidates individually unaudited.
- **7.2.7b — Native Faust proof of concept (partial; offline probes in review, [PR #81](https://github.com/wgabel/orbit2/pull/81)):** [measured research results and runnable harness](../instruments/faust-physical-model-probes.md) render the audited pluck/flute at 44.1/48 kHz, with silent idle, finite output, pitch/decay, bounded excitation and preliminary dynamic/throughput tests. The [calibrated expressive pluck wrapper](../instruments/faust-physical-pluck.md) passes 222 trials (37 chromatic pitches × 3 T60 × 2 rates), trigger pitch/velocity latching, optional bounded excitation and rapid-event stress; flute has lower-register mode/tuning failures at 48 kHz. Matched dry approximate references available; exact Orbit graph A/B, wider playable range, 7.3.4 comparative perf and human audition remain open. No shipped nodes.
- **7.2.7c — Reusable physical model and expression interface (planned; pluck contract prototyped offline in 7.2.7b):** integrate necessary primitives (waveguides, exciters, body/formant resonances) and parameterized cores through ordinary registry/emitters, typed note sources and editable composites. Expose continuous expression, pitch, gate/trigger and velocity where physically meaningful; no per-note recompiles; bounded parameter/state, serialization, validation, history and tests. First mono; use 7.2.6c for independent polyphony.
- **7.2.7d — Acoustic/electromechanical organ candidates:** independently prototype editable nine-drawbar Hammond and pipe-rank Principal/Gedackt/Reed using existing nodes; compare baseline and new candidates with sustained, repeated-note and chord profiles. Add key contact/percussion, leakage, foldback/shared tonewheels, rank-specific chiff/wind/resonance in increments where useful. Separate piano-like velocity from organ swell. See exact candidate recipes in the specification; a true pipe model needs distinct acoustic mechanisms.
- **7.2.7e — Reusable Rotary Speaker effect:** audition the current two-band LFO-modulated Gain/Delay composite recipe; implement a phase-compatible crossover, rotor-specific Doppler/amplitude motion, slow/fast/brake with independent ramp-up/down, cabinet coloring, clean bypass and bounded state. Mono first; stereo mic/pan after 7.5, saturation coordinated with 7.6.6. Verify crossover, sidebands, level, continuous speed changes and cost.
- **7.2.7f — Expand validated acoustic and modal models:** flute, clarinet, brass, bowed/plucked strings, didgeridoo/formant hybrids, guitar/harp, marimba, bells, djembe and measured handpan/gong banks as technically and legally cleared. Investigate strike position, modal coupling and Rhodes-like tine/pickup model; reserve complex piano and other cases for measured physical models or Phase 8 licensed sample/hybrid work. Use 7.6.2–7.6.5 for reproducible A/B, user approval and context tests; no claims of realism before audition.

#### Phase 7.6 — Instrument and sound design — repeatable audition (design/reference fixture available; remaining work planned)

Authoritative musical design and workflow: [instrument-design-and-audition.md](../examples/instrument-design-and-audition.md). Original case study: Prism Rush listener approval of its melody and composition, but rejection of its individual retro/childish timbres. Preserve successful scores and isolate sound design. This is a general-purpose workflow for any suitable voice, instrument, drone, texture, percussion or effect; the Prism Rush example is not a genre restriction. These increments do not require completion of all earlier phase numbers; (1)–(5) can begin with existing nodes now. Continue prioritizing 7.3.1, 7.3.2 and 7.3.4 before 7.5 and costly DSP expansions. Use the same benchmark approach for new processing and sample/polyphonic models.

- **7.6.1 — TEST melody driver and audition workspace (reference patch available; catalog integration planned):** [editable reference JSON](../examples/instrument-audition/reference.orbit2.json) supplies the fixed four-bar score, separate Pitch/Gate/Trigger/Velocity outputs, reference voice, A/B root mixer, annotations and TEST overview tag. Native render verifies 30 exact onsets over two cycles and score-matched controls. Reference asset/design: [PR #54](https://github.com/wgabel/orbit2/pull/54) (documentation, not runtime implementation). Register the example through the existing annotated example catalog and optionally as a `Testing` module when 7.4.3–7.4.5 ship. Ensure import/round trip, stable score, typed boundaries, safe level, search/outline discovery, no inner Master or duplicate clock; no new drone type.
- **7.6.2 — Shared instrument test interface and profiles (planned):** define reusable pitch reference, Gate/Trigger/Velocity inputs and Audio output conventions; use actual existing voice interfaces with explicit conversions. Add versioned low/bass, sustained pad/drone, repeated strike, drum, chord, modulation/long-duration, audio-input effect and contextual profiles as needed; use relevant controls only and preserve equivalent inputs and loudness during comparisons. Once 7.2.8d ships, migrate to real Note-mode patterns where useful without changing the frozen score. Prefer ordinary components and saved-patch copies until the Module Library is available.
- **7.6.3 — Candidate instruments and sounds across genres (planned):** build 10–15 contrasting editable instrument designs (initial 12 roles in the guide), as an initial batch, starting with expressive lead, electric/felt keys, rounded bass, strings/pad and percussion. Expand with genre-appropriate acoustic-inspired, experimental, retro, industrial and other sounds as needed. Use independent partial/transient/body shaping, ADSR, velocity-related tone, resonators and restrained ambience where useful. Prototype with existing engine; document dependencies and exact revisions. Distinguish musical effect from acoustic emulation. Do not certify a candidate from a render or waveform count.
- **7.6.4 — A/B audition, measured and listener-approved palette (planned):** keep the same suitable test profile and applicable score, onset/gate/velocity, register, gain-matched listening and dry/mix comparison; record native rendering, pitch, rest/tail, transient, peak, relative level and runtime-cost checks. Capture user feedback on intended tone, expressivity, texture and role suitability; chip character may be desired or undesired. Keep rejected/candidate versions separate; promote only approved specific revisions for stated roles and contexts, with optional multi-genre tags; agents consult the catalogue. Prefer multiple small test patches rather than a heavy all-voices live graph.
- **7.6.5 — Score-preserving production validation (planned):** compare sound palette revisions in representative arrangements across genres. First worked case: re-orchestrate Prism Rush with approved instruments, retain the same motifs, notes, harmonic/section plan, tempo, duration and dramatic breaks; render/compare original and revised stems, context balance, loudness-matched chorus/interlude/bridge, and clean ending. User listening is the acceptance evidence for improved timbre. Refine selected voices rather than rewriting the successful composition.
- **7.6.6 — Production dynamics and saturation (planned):** design and implement bounded nonlinear waveshaping/soft saturation with level compensation, anti-aliasing/oversampling analysis and dry/wet; detector-driven dynamics (envelope follower, compressor with threshold/ratio/attack/release/knee/makeup, then limiter with explicit sample/true-peak semantics and verified ceiling). Reuse existing effects/persistence/parameter/modulation/bypass model, benchmark state/cost and stereo compatibility, measure transfer curves, distortion, pumping, latency, peaks and click-safe parameter edits. Keep programmed faders distinct from compressors; deliver in independently testable increments. Coordinate with 8.7 envelope follower and 7.5 rather than duplicate those contracts.
- **7.6.7 — Dedicated complex synthesis follow-up (planned):** investigate bounded FM/phase-modulation operators with meaningful ratios/index and independent envelopes, evolving harmonic content and velocity control, and reusable operator templates.
