## Composition and musical analysis

- For musical patch, composition, analysis or feedback tasks, start with the [music creation index](../examples/README.md). It routes the end-to-end creative and validation workflow and distinguishes individual effects/voices from whole-piece arrangement. Read the relevant linked sections before proposing new guidance or creating a patch; check what is already documented before recommending additions. The index is not a required musical template or a constraint on genre, feature design, or exploration.
- For whole-piece planning and feedback about monotony, consult [compositional development](../examples/compositional-development.md). Evaluate micro (sound/gesture), meso (phrase/groove) and macro (form) development as relevant to the brief. When the desired contrast needs changes in musical material, plan and verify actual motifs, rhythms, harmony and section relationships rather than relying only on changing active layers. Preserve successful musical elements during refinement; intentional repetition and non-song forms remain valid.
- For cinematic, orchestral or hybrid soundtrack composition, **read [cinematic composition and orchestration](../examples/genres/cinematic-orchestration.md)** before building the graph. Plan the theme, harmonic foundation, distinct instrumental roles and register, evolving articulation, contrast and climax as applicable; use verified node contracts rather than assuming a DAW/sample-library feature exists. Treat the workflow as an adaptable technique, not a mandatory form for every cue.

- For harmony, tuning, melody, timbre, perceived loudness, spectral balance, depth and musical quality decisions, consult the relevant sections of the [music theory and electronic sound design guide](../examples/music-theory-and-sound-design.md). Use its examples and quality checks as adaptable musical guidance, not stylistic restrictions or evidence of listening verification. Check real node capabilities before building patches.
- Before writing a new pitched composition or substantial melodic/harmonic passage, **consult [harmony, scales and temperament](../examples/harmony-scales-and-temperament.md) and [classical composition and melody](../examples/classical-composition-and-melody.md)** during score planning. Select relevant techniques for the brief: identify a tonal/modal center or deliberate alternative, tuning, actual chord voicings/bass and independent voice paths, motif/phrase rhythm, cadence or other direction, and intentional development. Consider reduction to one keyboard-like timbre (bass + melody + complementary harmony/counterpoint), then elaborate and orchestrate; for classical inspiration study the cited scores and distinguish original exercises from transcriptions. Use the guides across genres without requiring classical rules or functional harmony for every piece. Validate written and actually sounded notes, durations and rests, vertical interactions, phase and voice-leading before production analysis; distinguish source research, proposed musical effects, measured evidence and listening feedback. Check the implementation limits before choosing tuning or polyphonic techniques.
- For evaluating why a piece works or fails, planning tension and release, developing listener expectations, or balancing familiarity with surprise, consult [musical quality, expectation and listener experience](../examples/musical-quality-and-expectation.md). Apply its research-informed questions in context, not as a universal quality score or a requirement for inaccessible listener-model tooling. Distinguish musical hypotheses, measured evidence and actual listener experience.
- For genre-oriented sound design or musical composition, start with the [electronic music genre index](../examples/electronic-music-genres.md). Select relevant genre families and [Orbit 2 musical techniques](../examples/genres/orbit-techniques.md) as useful, then follow the appropriate theory, arrangement and technical contracts. These are musical reference points, not an exhaustive genre list, required reading of every family, or constraints on novel music. Distinguish authentic production techniques from feasible approximations; verify current capabilities and the resulting music.

## Knowledge retention

- When asked to incorporate external music teaching material, follow [music learning source intake](../examples/music-learning-sources.md). Preserve provenance, explain the mechanism, decision and practical adaptation in the owning guide, provide a concrete example and limitations, and distinguish source-derived recommendations from verified Orbit implementations and listener approval. Avoid bare one-line rules, full transcript dumps and duplicate documentation. Read only the relevant source and existing topic guides.

- Maintain the [music creation index](../examples/README.md#maintain-this-entry-point) and [foundation catalogue](../examples/foundations/catalogue.md#maintaining-and-expanding-the-catalogue) when relevant knowledge changes. Record useful generalizable findings, actual reference assets and feedback with their evidence and limitations; retain previous revisions and link real PRs. If a new foundation is only a candidate, label it accordingly. For music-only tasks outside the scope of repository changes, report worthwhile additions for later rather than publishing assets or changing documentation without authorization.
