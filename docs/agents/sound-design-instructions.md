# Sound design, instrument selection and production instructions

[Global contributor rules](contributor-instructions.md) · [Task routing](task-routing.json)

Apply only the relevant conditional rules. These apply to sound design and production, and the realistic-instrument rule also applies to relevant Faust implementation work. Music tasks also use the [music instructions](music-instructions.md) where routed.

## Voices, instruments and sound quality

- When creating new music or designing/refining synthesized voices, **consult the [synthesizer patch recipes](../examples/synthesizer-patch-recipes.md)** during palette planning, before constructing the graph. Consider the eight reference recipes for the important musical roles and use, combine or adapt suitable techniques; record the chosen recipe(s) or why a different design better serves the brief. Verify node capabilities and audition/measure the adaptation where possible; the source recipes are not shipped or Orbit-validated presets. For finished voices and presets, use [sound quality and voice design](../examples/sound-quality-and-voice-design.md): avoid exposed raw noise and hollow/basic test-tone timbres in polished music unless musically intentional; shape noise, layer or filter the tonal source, articulate brightness and compare the voice in context. Pure sine is useful for a clean sub, partial or control signal, not a default foreground instrument. Protect the user's intended musical style and successful arrangement; improve the palette when feedback is about sound quality.
- **Before choosing sound generators for any new composition or substantial voice redesign, consult the [current instrument selection and routing recipes](../examples/instrument-recipes.md#choose-an-instrument-for-the-musical-role) and [implemented instrument usage](../usage/sound-and-instruments.md#faust-instrument-nodes)**, not just older oscillator-based recipes. Make a deliberate, role-based decision among the current Faust physical Pluck, Marimba, Clarinet, Flute, Violin (experimental), Brass, Electric Guitar, Drawbar Organ, Rotary Speaker effect, editable instrument composites, and basic synthesis. Check whether each voice needs Pitch + Trigger or Pitch + Gate, whether Velocity/Expression is optional, correct A4 reference and semitone routing, monophonic limitations, sample rate and CPU risk. Choose the best musical fit, not the newest node by default; audition a physical model and an appropriate synthetic alternative at comparable levels for important roles, and record why the selected source serves the brief. A merged implementation or successful native test is not evidence of acoustic realism or listener preference. Do not infer MIDI-like simultaneous polyphony, amplifier/cabinet simulation, or stereo routing from instrument names.
- For instrument and sound design, refinement or comparison in any genre, including feedback about unintended childish, chiptune/16-bit or overly synthetic timbres, **consult [instrument and sound design — repeatable audition](../examples/instrument-design-and-audition.md)** before choosing voices. Preserve approved melodies, harmony and form; develop spectrally and temporally distinct instruments, not just more similar waveform stacks or AD plucks. Choose a role-appropriate repeatable test profile; use the [TEST Melody Driver v1](../examples/instrument-audition/reference.orbit2.json) for pitched melodic instruments: fixed notes, gate, trigger and velocity, conservative level-matched A/B, role-appropriate register, solo and context tests. Use listener-approved palette revisions when appropriate; keep untested candidates explicitly labelled and record human feedback separately from technical measurements. For drums, drones, effects, textures and other sounds, use suitable fixed inputs instead of forcing the melody onto everything. The fixture is not a finished instrument; all intentional musical aesthetics remain valid.
- For realistic acoustic or electromechanical instrument design, **consult [Faust-first realistic instrument emulation](../instruments/realistic-instrument-emulation.md)**. Prefer appropriate cleared upstream physical-model functions over reinventing acoustic excitation from static waveforms; check exact pinned version, function and transitive licensing before integration, preserve existing preset IDs, and verify expressive behavior, native rendering, performance and matched-level listening. Use the existing-node organ and rotary recipes as candidates, not claims of acoustic realism; distinguish model-specific requirements from general-purpose synth design.
- For evolving voices and musical foundations, consider deliberate interactions between nodes: shared or offset modulation, overlapping partials, nonlinear shaping and different timescales can produce emergent harmonic shifts and coordinated breathing. Consult the [foundation catalogue](../examples/foundations/catalogue.md), beginning with the listener-validated breathing harmonic drone when relevant. Use principles where musically useful; no particular recipe, complexity or style is mandatory. Follow the catalogue's maintenance process for new discoveries, referencing the exact patch revision and keeping structural, audio, listener and in-context evidence distinct.

## Mixing and analysis

- For complete musical pieces and substantial sound-design work, use the [mixing, mastering and analysis guide](../examples/mixing-mastering-and-analysis.md). Where feasible, render a baseline (prefer the native engine; label independent previews), measure master and important voices across representative sections/full duration, identify problems, refine purposefully, then re-render and compare at matched levels. Check actual sound quality when listening is available; otherwise distinguish user listening feedback, objective measurements and hypotheses. Do not infer that a tone sounds good or noise is unobtrusive from RMS/peak alone, claim unsupported mastering processors, or require unavailable tooling to start creative work. Scale analysis to the task; report the evidence, changes and remaining limitations.
- When balancing melody, accents or other competing roles in a substantial piece, consult [contextual layer balance](../examples/contextual-layer-balance.md). Where feasible, compare aligned post-voice-fader roles against their simultaneous backing in representative short windows (including sparse and dense sections), preserve intentional rests and dynamics, and validate targeted changes on comparable windows. The research and case study offer diagnostics, not mandatory dB targets, equal-loudness rules or a requirement for unavailable tools.
