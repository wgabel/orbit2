# Music learning source intake and provenance

[Music creation index](README.md) · [Production workflow](production-workflow.md) · [Agent music policy](../agents/music-instructions.md)

This is the procedure for integrating user-supplied music tutorials, transcripts, articles and similar educational material into the project. It is mandatory when an agent is asked to retain such knowledge, not routine reading for every composition. Its goal is deeper practical guidance at low recurring context cost. Record useful techniques, not a collection of source transcripts.

## Intake procedure

1. **Inspect the supplied source itself.** Record the title, author/channel, publication date and canonical link when actually available, plus timestamp or line locations for important material. State missing details and whether the text is auto-generated, abridged or lacks audiovisual examples. Do not guess missing notes, settings, author names or links from the title. Keep third-party full transcripts outside the public repository unless their redistribution is authorized.
2. **Extract the teaching, not the marketing or tool setup.** Identify the specific problem, mechanism, technique, useful conditions and illustrative example. Discard gear shopping, promotions and DAW-specific steps that do not contribute to Orbit guidance. If a DAW feature teaches a useful musical technique, keep the technique while separating it from the original software. Treat beginner simplifications and stylistic advice as heuristics; identify relevant exceptions instead of promoting them to absolute rules.
3. **Reconcile with existing knowledge.** Read the music index, task index and actual closest owning topic guides. Merge new material into existing authoritative guides; link to existing technical instructions rather than copying port definitions. If a new focused topic merits a document, route it from the music index and applicable agent policy/configuration. Do not invent a second production workflow. Maintain the source register below and cite source IDs near adapted material.
4. **Write guidance that agents can reason with.** For each substantial technique, explain (a) the musical problem and why the approach may help, (b) how to recognize the situation in a score or patch, (c) a concrete sequence of decisions or steps, (d) an original worked example with notes, rhythms, roles or timings where relevant, (e) translation to current Orbit concepts or links to verified technical contracts, (f) failure modes, stylistic exceptions and a useful comparison or evaluation. A slogan such as "add variety" or a one-line table entry is not sufficient as the sole explanation. Tables and summaries are useful routing aids, not substitutes for reasoning. Keep each topic focused; detailed explanations should be loaded on demand by agents, not copied into the always-required bootstrap.
5. **Separate claims and evidence.** Source-derived means an educational source describes or motivates the approach; it is not independent proof. An Orbit candidate is a proposed adaptation, with assumptions and missing features identified. Orbit-verified means the exact patch/implementation revision, relevant technical checks and observed behavior are recorded. Listener-validated means actual human feedback on the stated example/revision and listening context is recorded. A native render or objective measurement does not equal listener approval. Do not upgrade evidence merely because another guide repeats it.
6. **Make the knowledge discoverable and maintainable.** Update the relevant task links and selective routes, preserve the previous sources/evidence, record actual PR provenance and any limitations. For new verified sound relationships use the existing [foundation catalogue](foundations/catalogue.md#maintaining-and-expanding-the-catalogue); otherwise keep compositional findings in their topic guide. Report what was added and what remains unverified. Future agents should not need to reread the source for normal composition.

## Source register

Initial intake: user-provided English auto-generated YouTube transcripts, delivered 2026-11-24 via DownSub text exports. Original video URLs, dates and channel identity were not included reliably in the supplied files; no external metadata verification was performed. The transcripts mark played audio as music rather than supplying a score or recording. The examples in Orbit guides are original exercises inspired by the concepts, not transcriptions or verified reconstructions. Title as supplied. Author/channel not reliably identified from the transcript.
