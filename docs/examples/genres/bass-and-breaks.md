# Bass music, experimental bass and broken rhythms

[Genre index](../electronic-music-genres.md) · [Orbit techniques](orbit-techniques.md) · [Theory](../music-theory-and-sound-design.md) · [Dance and club](club-and-dance.md)

In bass-oriented music the low end is often the main musical actor. Separate the stable, largely clean low fundamental from the much more animated audible mid-bass harmonics. Rhythm emerges from the relationship between short drums, bass gestures and silence. A convincing heavy piece need not be clipping, continuously loud or indiscriminately distorted. Design a strong sparse rhythm and bass phrase before adding complexity. UK and Jamaican sound-system cultures, hip-hop, jungle, dubstep and other scenes have different roots; shared production vocabulary does not make them interchangeable.

## Styles and construction

| Style | Typical grid and materials | Construction and evolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experimental bass | Variable 60–180; sub, unusual modulated voices, fragmented beats | Keep a clean anchor; alternate varied sculptural bass sounds with gaps, drum gestures and contrasting atmospheres. Not a fixed tempo/beat formula. |
| Freeform bass (descriptor) | Variable; psychedelic, warped, morphing timbres | Nonstandard phrase lengths, unexpected rhythmic shifts, call/response textures and abrupt/subtle contrast. |
| Leftfield bass | Variable; stripped broken grooves, peculiar timbres | Intentionally unfamiliar accents and sound relationships; build tension via timing, timbre and omission. |
| Deep dubstep | 135–145 half-time; sub, sparse snare, space | Heavy sustained or syncopated sine bass, backbeat around beat 3, restrained high percussion, occasional dub echoes. |
| UK dubstep | 135–145; swung/skipping rhythms, sub pressure | Dark bass phrases with space around clipped percussion and dub/garage roots; vary syncopation rather than requiring a beat-3 snare every bar. |
| Brostep | 140–150; aggressive mid-bass, strong drums | Dramatic drops, alternating growl-like timbres and rhythmic stops; dedicated sub follows phrase, not every bright modulation. |
| Riddim dubstep | 140–150; repeated syncopated bass cells | Repeat short, rhythmically defined bass figures with small sound changes and sparse percussion. |
| Tearout dubstep | 140–155; forceful short bass stabs, noisy attacks | Interlock stabs/gaps with hard impacts and breaks; use quieter lead-in to create power rather than pushing gain. |
| Melodic dubstep | 128–150; emotive chords, sub and articulated lead | Harmony and hook remain audible through halftime drops; alternate energetic bass section and gentler chord-driven break. |
| Future garage | 120–140; soft chords, shuffled 2-step, subs | Space between displaced kicks, syncopated hats and abstract vocal-like fragments; introspective harmony. |
| Wave | 65–85 or 130–170; atmospheric minor chords, 808-like bass | Long sliding bass notes, trap-influenced drums and a spacious lead; swap between sparse breakdown and full bass entry. |
| Hardwave | 130–170; wave atmosphere, more forceful leads/drums | Bright detuned melodic hook over sustained sub, harder transients and deliberate drop contrast. |
| Halftime bass | 150–180 grid or perceived 75–90; deep sub, drums at half-time | Bass phrasing and double-time hats contrast a slow heavy snare grid; change the response each phrase. |
| Glitch hop | 85–115; hip-hop/funk backbeat, chopped/stuttering timbres | Short syncopated pitched riff, rhythmic interruptions, swung percussion, glitch accents. |
| Midtempo bass | 85–115; mechanical pulse, dark modulated bass | Stomping kick/snare or straight pulse, repeating motif with small rhythmic and filter mutations; use intensity contrast. |
| Wonky / post-dubstep | 90–140; uneven synth riffs, broken beat and bass | Deliberately off-center accents, unusual harmonies, conflicting subdivisions and warm/digital colors. |
| Bass house | 120–130; four-on-floor, bass hook and claps | Offbeat or syncopated bass lead answers kick; automate riff and sub level together; cross to [house](club-and-dance.md). |
| Trap instrumental | 130–160 or perceived 65–80; deep 808-like tone, snare and hats | Tuned bass with glides and gaps, half-time backbeat and subdivisions, small minor-key motif. |
| Hybrid trap | 140–160; trap drums plus heavy sound-design bass | Sparse bass stabs/drops, call/response, buildup and bass switch; preserve kick/sub separation. |
| Future bass | 130–160; lush detuned chords, melodic hook, trap-like drums | Bright chord rhythm with pronounced amplitude pumping, rising chord inversion or hook, contrasting soft break. |
| Grime instrumental | Around 140; sparse syncopated drums, angular synth motifs | Wide gaps for an MC or lead; stark minor riffs, short aggressive stabs and deep but restrained bass. |
| Purple sound | Around 140; dubstep rhythm, bright synth/funk harmony | Expressive portamento motifs, bass groove, colorful chords and offbeat stabs. |
| Drum and bass (umbrella) | 160–180; rapid breakbeat feel, low bass | Strong snare anchors and syncopated kick, steady hats, distinct sub and changing bass layers; alternate drum density. |
| Jungle | 155–175; chopped syncopated breaks, dub bass | Break edits, snare displacement and rolling low end, often reggae/dub-influenced accents; synthesize hits if samples unavailable. |
| Atmospheric/intelligent jungle | 155–175; rolling breaks, floating pads and sub | Extended chord ambience, break variations and deep bass; keep drums lighter beneath the melodic layers. |
| Liquid drum and bass | 165–180; fluid bass, warm/jazzy/soulful chords | Musical chord and melody phrasing over rolling drums, sub supports roots and transitions rather than constant distortion. |
| Minimal/deep D&B | 165–180; sparse breaks and weighty bass | Fewer drum accents, tightly designed rhythmic sub motifs and small texture changes; emphasize dynamics and negative space. |
| Rollers (D&B descriptor) | 165–180; persistent rolling groove, repeated bass | Drive a continuous rhythmic cycle with small variations and alternate bass call/response; not a single fixed subgenre. |
| Neurofunk | 170–180; precise drums, complex evolving bass | Detuned/inharmonic mid-bass, spectral movement and intricate call/response; tight separate sub, evolving pattern across bars. |
| Techstep | 165–180; darker tech textures, mechanical beats | Sparse industrial timbres, repetitive riff and ominous bass pressure; rhythmic contrast over lush harmony. |
| Jump-up D&B | 165–180; bouncy short bass hook, strong snare | Memorable high/mid-bass figure with repeated pitches and breaks; use clear rhythmic response and drop contrast. |
| Darkstep | 165–185; dense dark breaks, uneasy textures | Aggressive low/mid-bass and varied tension, dark atmospheric intro versus fast drum-driven section. |
| Crossbreed | 170–200; D&B broken rhythm with hardcore kick language | Contrast fast breaks and pitched kick tails; carefully allocate low-frequency lanes. |
| Drumfunk | 160–180; intricate break edits, subtler sub | The drum pattern is the lead; overlapping cycles, irregular accents and small dynamic changes, bass provides continuity. |
| Breakcore | 170–240 or variable; fragmented fast breaks, contrasts | Sudden beat edits, shifts and complex percussion, pitched or sampled fragments; exact sample slicing not yet available. |
| Drill'n'bass | 150–210 or variable; hyper-detailed broken percussion | Rapid subdivision changes, timbral surprises and deliberate asymmetric rhythms over odd melodies. |
| Footwork / juke | Around 160; repeated short drum/bass cells, syncopation | Interlocking kick patterns, displaced claps, repeated vocal-like fragments and gaps; build around Chicago dance rhythm, not generic D&B. |
| UK garage / 2-step | 125–140; shuffled hats, displaced kick, short bass | Use broken kick grid and strong backbeat, chords or stabs, swing by offsetting accents rather than adding random hits. |
| Speed garage | 125–140; four-on-floor or skipping beat, heavy bass | Garage percussion and sustained/reese-like bass; syncopated hook against steady pulse. |
| Breakbeat | 105–145 or variable; broken dance groove | Kick/snare patterns rather than kick every beat; repetition with fills and bass interaction. |
| Nu skool breaks | 125–140; propulsive break and synthetic riff | Modulated bass riff, dry break transients, contrasting breakdowns. |
| Big beat | 95–135; loud-feeling funky breaks, riffs | Bold simple hooks, backbeat and sampled-style accents; bigger events through dynamic contrast. |
| Dub / dubwise | Often 60–90 (or doubled); bass guitar-like line, offbeats and echoes | Bass is melodic foundation, space is rhythmic; mute parts to reveal tail interplay. Dub has Jamaican origins and is not inherently an electronic genre. |

## Practical design of experimental bass voices

Start with [T4 clean sub](orbit-techniques.md#t4--sub-and-kick-foundation), [T6 mid-bass](orbit-techniques.md#t6--moving-mid-bass-and-metallic-tones), and [T3 rhythm](orbit-techniques.md#t3--rhythm-grid-patterns-and-rests). Build a small palette of complementary sounds with different envelopes, not one maximal sound:

| Voice | Construction | Musical role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stable sub | Sine at F1 (~43.65 Hz) or G1 (~49 Hz), pitch pattern and smooth dedicated amplitude/gain lane | Weight; simplify melodic contour if competing with kick. |
| Detuned Reese-like voice | Two saws near F2, slight opposed tuning, mixer, high-pass around 110–160 Hz, low-pass modulated by LFO/AD | Slowly expanding “call”; no true stereo required; filter keeps sub separate. |
| Growl-like filter voice | Saw/square, band-pass varying around 400–1800 Hz, controlled Q, AD on gain; parallel second band at lower level if needed | Short sharper “response”; spectral/rhythmic articulation replaces unsupported wavetable/distortion chain. |
| Metallic short voice | Sine carrier with audio-rate Hz modulation from another oscillator, tapered AD, high-pass | Inharmonic punctuation; choose frequency depth/ratio for tone and avoid unintended frequency clamps. |
| Air and debris | White/pink noise into band-pass or high-pass, brief AD, optionally short delay | Tiny transitions and percussion, not permanent broadband layer. |

Example phrase at 140 BPM: count one bar as 16 sixteenth-note slots. Kick on 1 and 8, snare on 9, hats on selected 3/7/11/15 slots. Let a sub strike on 1, support the call on 4–6, leave 8–9 for drums, respond on 11–13 and leave 16 empty. Re-evaluate overlap by ear. The patterns are separate control lanes; a Note Sequencer Hold does not mute anything. See [T3](orbit-techniques.md#t3--rhythm-grid-patterns-and-rests) for exact encoding of real gaps and [T7](orbit-techniques.md#t7--percussion-and-impact) for synthesized drum construction. For neurofunk, change pitch/rhythm/timbre between the call and response; for deep dubstep, simplify both drastically and emphasize sub/space. For a heavy sound, judge its relationship to silence and rhythm, not just instantaneous loudness.

### Whole-piece sketch: 140 BPM experimental bass

| Bars | Musical function | Changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–16 | Introduce world | Filtered atmosphere, motif, distant percussion; low end withheld. |
| 17–32 | Establish tension | Establish pulse, short bass hints, rising cutoff; thin out just before entry. |
| 33–48 | First bass section | Sub + drum anchors, alternating bass calls with audible gaps. |
| 49–64 | Variation | Change response timbre, displace final hats, introduce one new pitch contour. |
| 65–80 | Breakdown | Remove heavy rhythm/sub; reveal original harmony/motif and trail. |
| 81–96 | Strongest return | More pointed call/response, new accents and controlled harmonic twist. |
| 97–112 | Outro | Reduce density, end sub and drum phrases, let the final effect tail decay. |

At 140 BPM this is about 3:12 before extra tail; extend by composing additional contrasting passages, not duplicating the entire loop unchanged. Use [form controls](orbit-techniques.md#t9--long-form-arrangement) or manual performance. Current engine lacks general swing/beat-slicing, wavetable and dedicated nonlinear resampling, true stereo and automatic sidechain detection; approximate selectively and disclose limitations. Do not promise authentic complex break samples, vowel-formant resynthesis or mastering effects without implementation evidence.
