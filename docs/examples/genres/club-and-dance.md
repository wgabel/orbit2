# Dance and club music

[Genre index](../electronic-music-genres.md) · [Orbit techniques](orbit-techniques.md) · [Bass and breaks](bass-and-breaks.md)

Dance music often derives its identity from timing, repetition, physical energy and the evolution of a few rhythmic cells. A strong kick/bass relationship and compelling groove matter more than a large patch. Start with a recognizable one- or two-bar pattern, then design phrase-level variations, changes in density and sections. For a DJ-oriented piece, an intro/outro with stable pulse may be useful; for a shorter narrative piece, a different structure may work better.

## House and disco descendants

| Style | Typical grid and materials | Construction and evolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chicago house | 115–130; four-on-floor, claps, bass, piano-like chords | Kick every quarter, claps on 2/4, offbeat hats, repeating soulful chord/bass motif and gradual arrangement. |
| Deep house | 115–125; soft chord voicings, warm bass | Jazzy seventh/ninth chords and restrained stabs; let groove and harmonic turns carry long sections. |
| Acid house | 115–130; resonant sliding single-note bass | Repeated short saw/square pitch pattern with cutoff/Q motion and accents; simulate slides with glide. |
| Jackin' house | 120–130; swung drums and funky short riffs | Bouncy syncopation, repetitive chopped-style stabs and groove variations. |
| Funky/disco house | 115–130; bass groove and bright chord fragments | Syncopated bass, guitar/string-like stabs, upbeat hats and repeating hook. |
| Vocal/diva house | 115–130; chord/rhythm support for powerful lead | Songlike phrasing with intro, verse and chorus; synth melody can stand in for the voice but not actual singing. |
| Garage house | 115–130; soulful harmony and shuffled percussion | Short chord voicings, funky bass, subtle hat displacement and space for melody. |
| Progressive house | 120–130; evolving chords, arps, steady pulse | Gradual layered build and harmonic development over many bars. Festival variants favor bigger melodic climaxes and breaks. |
| Tech house | 120–130; minimal harmony, drum groove and bass hook | Small bass loop, short repeated stabs, percussion exchange and strategic muting. |
| Minimal / deep tech house | 118–130; reduced drum and sub vocabulary | Sparse rhythmic bass/chord hits, shifting micro-details, long gradual changes. |
| Organic house | 105–125; softer kick, organic percussion, acoustic-like color | Flowing melodic motif, warm layered texture and subtle evolving percussion. |
| Afro house | 115–125; layered percussion, repeating bass/chords | Interlocking asymmetric rhythmic cells, deep groove and longer phrases; avoid substituting generic four-on-floor for its percussion identity. |
| Afro tech | 120–130; Afro house groove plus darker electronic tones | Percussion interplay, focused bass and hypnotic electronic responses. |
| Tribal house | 120–135; layered toms and percussion | Interlocking pitched/noise drums and cyclic accents; vary density in sections. |
| Electro house | 125–135; bright heavy bass, simple hook | Four-on-floor and foreground riff, sparse chord support, strong drop; not Detroit electro. |
| Big room house | 125–132; dramatic lead and long kick | Large contrast between sparse buildup/break and simple strong drop hook. |
| Slap house | 118–128; bouncy short bass plucks, melodic hook | Syncopated bass hits with short envelopes, stable kick and simple songlike sections. |
| Nu-disco | 105–125; disco bass, warm chords and synth strings | Funky bass and layered chord accents, steady groove, long melodic phrases. |
| French/filter house | 115–130; repeating funk/disco-like phrase | Rhythmic filtering and level dynamics on a short harmonic loop; synthesize original phrase instead of sampled disco. |

## Techno and related music

| Style | Typical grid and materials | Construction and evolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detroit techno | 120–140; machine rhythm, futuristic melody and chord color | Repeating drum/bass framework with expressive chords or lead; variation in machine timbres and phrase structure. |
| Minimal techno | 120–135; sparse clicks, kick and bass | Reduce material to a few elements; small changes in timbre and timing make repetition expressive. |
| Hypnotic/deep techno | 120–145; repeating modal figures, subdued percussion | Interlocking cycles and subtle spectral motion; hold harmony long enough for texture to become the lead. |
| Dub techno | 110–140; recurring chord stab, kick, delays | Minor/seventh/add-note voicing, low-pass motion and alternating echo density; long sections with restrained additions. |
| Acid techno | 130–150; fast acid patterns, driving kick | Resonant saw/square monophonic riff, glides and accents, controlled filter builds. |
| Peak-time/driving techno | 125–145; forceful kick, rolling bass and hook | Constant pulse with intensifying rhythmic layers, tension breaks, return to full groove. |
| Hard techno | 140–160+; pounding kick, dark riff | Strong short low-end events and searing upper texture; contrast density, not just volume. |
| Industrial techno | 125–155; metallic textures, machine hits | Noise and inharmonic accents against rigid or broken kick rhythm; eerie recurring motifs. |
| Schranz | 140–160+; dense rhythmic loops and hard kick | Layer repeated percussive cells for intensity; carve distinct bands, introduce breaks to reset attention. |
| Melodic techno | 120–135; emotional arps, steady pulse and evolving bass | Minor/modal progression, melodic motif and slow build/release; avoid overfilling a large reverb bed. |

## Trance, psychedelic and faster dance music

| Style | Typical grid and materials | Construction and evolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Progressive trance | 126–138; bass pulse, layered melody and pads | Slowly evolving arpeggios and harmony, build towards recurring theme. |
| Uplifting trance | 136–145; rapid bass, bright detuned leads, sweeping chords | Energetic grid, breakdown revealing emotional chord progression, rising lead and full thematic return. |
| Tech trance | 135–145; tighter drums and stabs with trance tension | Rhythmic synth hook and driving bass, contrasting atmospheric/trance break. |
| Acid trance | 130–150; acid riff and expansive harmony | Resonant patterned bass with glide, filter movement and swelling pads. |
| Goa trance | 135–150; interlocking melodic acid/arp lines | Cyclic modal motifs and intertwining layers, long psychedelic development. |
| Progressive psytrance | 130–142; kick, offbeat/rolling bass, evolving FX | Steady hypnotic groove with gradual layer exchange, carefully timed low end. |
| Full-on psytrance | 140–150; rapid rolling bass, bright FX and melodic snippets | Alternating fast bass pulses between kicks, short spatial accents, energetic phrase changes. |
| Darkpsy | 145–165+; dense rolling bass and strange textures | Dark tonal or atonal material, unusual timbral events over persistent rhythm. |
| Forest psytrance | 145–160; organic-feeling dark textures and bass | Interwoven bubbling/noise details, evolving environments and subterranean groove. |
| Hi-tech psytrance | 170–200+; extremely fast rhythm and sharp synthetic edits | Rapid sound changes, odd cells and abrupt phrase switching; keep timing and low-end clarity at speed. |
| Hard trance | 140–155; bright resonant leads and forceful bass | Repeated dramatic melodic phrases, tension breaks and powerful kick return. |
| Hardstyle | 145–155; pitched distorted kick and anthem-like leads | Kick tail acts as bass; melodic breakdown and strong rhythmic drop. Use tuned synthetic kick approximations, not nonexistent distortion. |
| Rawstyle | 150–165; abrasive kick types, dark screeches | Contrasting kick pitches/tails and sharply separated lead stabs, dramatic gaps. |
| Happy hardcore / UK hardcore | 160–180; upbeat hook, fast kick, bright chords | Simple energetic songlike melody, fast chord accompaniment and breakdown/drop. |
| Gabber / early hardcore | 160–200; assertive hard kick, short synth riffs | Repeating forceful kick/bass and brief hook changes; keep safe headroom. |
| Industrial hardcore | 160–220; harsh kick, dark noisy textures | Mechanical rhythms, sharp contrast and tightly carved distorted-style timbres. |
| Frenchcore | 180–220; pitched hard kicks, bouncy bass and melody | Strong repeated kick/bass cell, alternating melodic themes and driving sections. |

## Electro, disco and regional dance traditions

| Style | Typical grid and materials | Construction and evolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detroit electro | 120–145; broken drum-machine rhythms, robotic bass | Syncopated kicks, crisp snare and hats, arpeggiated futuristic melody; not electro house. |
| EBM (electronic body music) | 110–140; sequenced bass, rigid drums and industrial color | Strident repeated single-note bass and muscular backbeat; sparse aggressive gestures. |
| New beat | 90–115; slow heavy dance rhythm, dark synths | Sluggish repeating bass, sparse stabs and ominous texture. |
| Italo disco | 105–125; octave bass, synth chords, romantic melody | Repeating bass ostinato and contrasting verse/chorus-like motifs. |
| Eurodance | 125–150; driving rhythm, hook, bright chords | Clear singable lead, bass/kick and large refrain contrast. |
| Indie dance | 100–130; alternative-song harmony and club rhythm | Repeating bass hook, expressive synth tones, songlike and extended groove structures. |
| Amapiano | 105–115; syncopated percussion, chords, pitched log-drum-like bass | Interlocking groove, delayed/answering low pitched percussion and soft keys; space around the bass melody. |
| Gqom | 120–130; sparse ominous synths and syncopated kicks | Broken asymmetric low percussion cells with chant-like/short responding motifs; not a standard house kick pattern. |
| Kuduro | 130–150; sharp syncopated percussion and riffs | Layer complementary accent cells and driving bass; dance groove is central. |
| Baile funk / funk carioca | 120–150+; signature syncopated drum cells, bass and short motifs | Repetitive tamborzão-derived or newer beat vocabulary, calls and responses; different styles have distinct grids. |
| Dembow | 85–115 or doubled; repeating syncopated drum pattern | Strong kick/snare-derived rhythmic cell, repeating bass/short melodic hook; focus on actual rhythmic accents. |
| Electronic cumbia | 80–110 or variable; cumbia rhythm, bass and melodic fragments | Characteristic alternating percussion and bass, melodic stabs, dub/club fusion where desired. |
| Jersey club | 130–145; syncopated repeated kick clusters, chopped fragments | Triplet-like kick gestures and call/response hooks, short repeated chopped-style tones. |
| Baltimore club | 125–140; break-derived kick/snare and repeated hooks | Syncopated drum loop and short repeated phrases with variation through rhythmic cuts. |

Regional scenes have distinct dance, cultural and production histories; these compact recipes are starting points for research and composition, not substitutes for studying their grooves or listening to artists.

## Translate dance patterns to Orbit 2

- Use [T3 rhythm](orbit-techniques.md#t3--rhythm-grid-patterns-and-rests) for shared synchronized sixteenth-note steps. Try a 16-slot grid: four-on-floor kick 1/5/9/13, clap 5/13, hat 3/7/11/15. Change this deliberately for broken-beat styles. Align sub rests to kick, not every style requires sidechain-like pumping.
- [T4 sub/kick](orbit-techniques.md#t4--sub-and-kick-foundation), [T7 percussion](orbit-techniques.md#t7--percussion-and-impact) for weight and transient distinction. [T6 mid-bass](orbit-techniques.md#t6--moving-mid-bass-and-metallic-tones) for acid, mechanical and aggressive tones.
- [T1 chords](orbit-techniques.md#t1--harmonic-beds-and-pads) and [T2 motif](orbit-techniques.md#t2--melody-plucks-and-accents) for arps, chord stabs and lead. Pitch patterns transpose an entire bank at unity; independent voicings require independent pitch lanes or pre-tuned voices.
- [T8 space](orbit-techniques.md#t8--delay-reverb-and-depth) for dub techno, trance breaks and evolving stabs, with dry kick/sub. [T9 form](orbit-techniques.md#t9--long-form-arrangement) for arranging energy: e.g. 16 bars intro → 16 establish groove → 16 build → 8 break → 16 strongest groove → 16 outro. Make transitions on phrase boundaries; keep an intentional ending.
