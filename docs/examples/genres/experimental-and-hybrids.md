# Experimental electronic music and crossovers

[Genre index](../electronic-music-genres.md) · [Ambient](ambient.md) · [Bass](bass-and-breaks.md) · [Orbit techniques](orbit-techniques.md)

Experimentation can happen at the level of timbre, tuning, processing, rhythm, form or even the composition process itself. Choose an audible idea and make its development meaningful; unusual sounds or randomization do not by themselves create a compelling piece. A stable recurring element can make experimental variation more intelligible. Conversely, an intentionally non-tonal composition can be organized by texture, rhythmic gestures and dynamic contrast instead of chords.

## Experimental approaches

| Style or approach | Typical materials and pulse | Construction and evolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IDM | Variable, often 80–150; intricate beats, synthetic motifs | Detailed percussion and unusual rhythmic variation against a recognizable harmonic/melodic anchor. |
| Braindance (overlapping scene/descriptor) | Variable; acid-like voices, complex rhythm, eccentric melody | Interleaving melodic cells and rapid rhythm/timbre changes; often playful contrasts. |
| Electronica (umbrella) | Variable; electronic voices, rhythmic and nonrhythmic passages | Combine song, beat and textural structures; select a coherent sound palette rather than treating “electronica” as a single formula. |
| Glitch | Free or grid; short discontinuous digital-like events | Interrupt repeated figures with stutters, silences and altered transients; keep clicks intentional and controlled. |
| Microsound | Free; extremely short tones and tiny noise events | Density, pitch scatter and envelope shape form larger gestures; approximate clouds with tapered events, no actual granular processor. |
| Musique concrète | Free; recorded objects and transformed sounds | Compose via juxtaposition and transformation of recorded material; Orbit can model contrasting synthetic gestures but not true recorded-sound montage yet. |
| Electroacoustic | Free or variable; acoustic/electronic timbres | Organize contrasting sonic gestures, resonance, density, and space; no requirement of regular pulse or tonal center. |
| Acousmatic (approach) | Free; sound sources obscured or abstracted | Shape apparent motion, distance, gesture and scene changes; mono depth is an approximation of spatial composition. |
| Noise music | Free or variable; noise spectra and non-tonal textures | Change bandwidth, persistence, density and dynamic arcs; silence and quieter contrast add meaning. |
| Harsh noise | Free; dense broadband/inharmonic texture | Rapid shifts in spectrum and intensity, contrasted with pinched narrow bands; evoke aggression without unsafe playback or clipping. |
| Harsh noise wall | Free; largely continuous dense noise | Very slow variation or deliberate apparent stasis; use filtered noise at safe levels and give the listener an intentional entry/exit. |
| Industrial electronic | Variable; repetitive machinery, noise and rhythm | Mechanical cycles, harsh stabs, sparse motifs and dynamic shocks. See also industrial ambient and industrial techno. |
| Power electronics | Free or mechanical pulse; abrasive high/low tones, noise | Confrontational timbral gestures, contrasting registers and abrupt changes; keep output controlled. |
| Deconstructed club | Variable, often club-related grid; fragments and dramatic silence | Displace expected kick/snare and phrase structure, juxtapose sounds and retain only selected dance cues. |
| Algorithmic / generative electronic (process) | Free or clocked; rules, interacting cycles | Define pitch and density constraints, allow cycles to align/disalign, impose long-range dynamic form. No assumption of random algorithms. |
| Minimal electronic / process music | Free or steady; tiny set of repeating cells | Phase displacement, gradual addition/subtraction or one slowly changing control, exposing the process to the listener. |
| Berlin school / kosmische electronic | Free/slow intro, often 80–130 sequenced pulse | Develop long repeating arpeggios against evolving pads and solo motif; layer changes over several minutes. |
| Electroacoustic improvisation (performance approach) | Free; spontaneous gesture and sound response | Prepare controllable voices and shared sound palette, use manual triggers, live pitch and gain performance; record/plan separately if needed. |

## Melodic, downtempo and psychedelic crossovers

| Style | Typical grid and materials | Construction and evolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downtempo | 60–110; relaxed broken groove, soft harmony | Slow drum/bass pocket, repeated melody and color shifts, space between phrases. |
| Chillout | Free or 70–110; gentle pulse, comforting textures | Consonant/modal harmony and easy-moving bass; subtle beat variation and soft transitions. |
| Balearic | Variable 80–125; eclectic relaxed dance and pop textures | Loose groove, warmth, melodic harmony and breathing arrangement; not defined by one tempo. |
| Trip hop | 65–100; slow heavy break, melancholic harmony | Dusty-feeling drums, syncopated bass, haunting motif and spacious contrast; synthesize original phrases rather than sample recordings. |
| Lo-fi hip-hop / chillhop | 65–95; jazzy chords, muted backbeat | Soft extended chord loop, slight timbral drift, sparse bass and melodic variations; imperfect feel without excessive noise. |
| Instrumental hip-hop | 70–105 or doubled; break/drum-machine groove, bass | Pocket, syncopated stabs and head-nod accents; leave space for melody or a hypothetical MC. |
| Psybient | Free or 60–110; evolving space textures and psychedelic pulses | Long ambient form with subtle rhythm, resonant color changes and abstract events; contrast calm and strange. |
| Psychill | 75–120; psychedelic detail, mellow rhythmic movement | Syncopated drums and bass, layered arps and changing effects; groove more prominent than pure ambient. |
| Psydub | 65–110; dub bass, offbeats, psychedelic echoes | Bass-led syncopation and delay as musical counterpoint, unusual modulation and sparse distant accents. |
| Ambient jazz crossover | Free or 60–100; extended harmony and loose phrasing | Independent upper voices, ninths/elevenths and subtle timing offset; modal improvisatory gestures over a pedal. |
| Synthwave / retrowave | 80–120; analog-style chords, steady drums, octave bass | Nostalgic harmonic hook, gated-feeling snare/tail, contrasting song sections; use detuned waves and chorus. |
| Darksynth | 85–135; dark synth ostinato, mechanical rhythm | Strong repeated minor riff, layered aggressive top bass and pulsing drums; combine breaks with sustained cinematic tension. |
| Synthpop | 95–135; hook melody, synth bass, chord and drum-machine parts | Verse/pre-chorus/chorus contrast, memorable motif and changing chord density; synth instrumental is an adaptation of vocal form. |
| Electropop | 90–135; electronic hook and strong pop rhythm | Clear lead and song sections, contrasting sparse verses and bright layers at refrain. |
| Hyperpop | Variable; exaggerated synthetic textures, abrupt changes | Bright melodic fragments, distorted-style bass, strong dynamics and sudden shifts; voice processing is not reproduced by synth alone. |
| Glitch pop | 85–145; pop melody with edited electronic textures | Recurrent song hook interrupted by rhythmic/sonic micro-edits and gaps. |
| Chillwave | 75–110; soft-focus synth chords, retro colors, pulse | Melodic chord loops with drift, filtering and chorus/delay; keep a clear motif in the haze. |
| Vaporwave | Free or slow 60–100; slowed/recontextualized nostalgia, unusual loop form | Original jazz-influenced chords and smeared echoes can evoke its character; not actual sample manipulation. |
| Chiptune / chip-inspired | Variable; square/triangle pulse, noise drums | Constrained voice count, fast arpeggiated chords, simple catchy melodic shapes and short envelopes; not hardware emulation or bitcrushing. |
| Spacesynth | 100–135; melodic sequencers and spacious synth leads | Strong octave bass and repeated sequenced motif, luminous changes and instrumental dance structure. |

## Design with constraints

Choose a primary compositional constraint, e.g. five pitches; one melodic oscillator with a continuously changing modulation ratio; 5-step and 7-step interlocking rhythms; or just three sound objects in contrasting registers. Select a stable relationship (pedal, motif, recurring pulse, timbral signature), then vary one or two axes. For an acousmatic or electroacoustic-inspired work use abstract synthetic gestures with distinct attack, duration, spectrum and depth. For glitch/IDM combine [rhythm grids](orbit-techniques.md#t3--rhythm-grid-patterns-and-rests), [percussion](orbit-techniques.md#t7--percussion-and-impact), short [texture gestures](orbit-techniques.md#t5--noise-and-textural-gestures) and melodic phrasing. For synthwave use [harmonic beds](orbit-techniques.md#t1--harmonic-beds-and-pads), [lead motifs](orbit-techniques.md#t2--melody-plucks-and-accents) and [space](orbit-techniques.md#t8--delay-reverb-and-depth).

Example of a five-minute experimental hybrid: 0–60 s introduce three recognizably different sonic objects (soft tuned bell, low filtered noise swell and short mechanical chirp); 60–120 s reveal a quiet 5-step/7-step rhythm sharing a sixteenth-note step; 120–180 s establish a simple repeating four-note melodic figure; 180–240 s dissolve the rhythm into isolated metallic hits and an airy chord; 240–300 s return a transformed original bell figure, then let noise and the final echo disappear. Assign independent lanes, keep density under control, and use [T9](orbit-techniques.md#t9--long-form-arrangement) for macro form. Avoid making all parameter modulation unrelated to the piece's audible purpose.
