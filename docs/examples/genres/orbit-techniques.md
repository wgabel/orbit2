These are composable graph recipes using the currently documented engine, not new nodes, persisted presets, exact genre emulations or guarantees of musical quality. Values are conservative starting points. Read the relevant linked contract when building a graph, especially when generating its serialized representation. Verify names, units, modes, active sockets, ranges, signal types and implementation status against the current code. Reuse voice graphs where possible; the planned custom module library is not a prerequisite to using ordinary composites.

Treat basic oscillator recipes as scaffolding. ## T1 — Harmonic beds and pads

**Use for:** ambient, dub chords, trance pads, liquid D&B, synthwave. Choose a musical register and voicing using [theory](../music-theory-and-sound-design.md#3-scales-harmony-and-tonal-color). For D minor/add9, a possible spread is D2 (73.42 Hz), A3 (220 Hz), F4 (349.23 Hz), E5 (659.26 Hz). Use separate Oscillators in Free Hz mode, gentle amplitudes (e.g. 0.10, 0.06, 0.04, 0.025), mix to a Filter and local Gain. Detune one quiet partner by a few cents for slow beating (ratio `2^(cents/1200)`); add a triangle/saw at small amplitude for audible cutoff motion. Sine at the fundamental alone has no large upper-harmonic spectrum to filter away. Use a slow sine (e.g. 0.013 Hz, amplitude 1) into low-pass cutoff base 1000 Hz with amount 600 Hz, Q 0.7. Chorus at subtle depth or reverb can soften a bed; avoid washing out the harmony.

For parallel chord movement, connect a Note Sequencer Pitch to each free oscillator's Semitones-tracked Pitch at unity (reference defines zero shift, not base frequency). For independent voice leading, use separate tuned oscillators and pitch lanes with the same step timing. Choose target notes relative to each voice's reference; don't send literal frequencies to Pitch. A shared transposition does not change chord quality. If using a continuous pad, long glides can work; for discrete chords, keep glide short and shape attacks. For muted dub stabs, put an AD-controlled Gain (base 0) after the chord mixer, then delay/reverb and an arrangement Gain. Use [T3](#t3--rhythm-grid-patterns-and-rests) for selective strikes; [T8](#t8--delay-reverb-and-depth) for depth.

Contracts: [oscillators](../../audio/oscillators-and-noise.md), [pitch control](../../instruments/pitch-control.md), [Note Sequencer](../../instruments/note-sequencer.md), [effects](../../audio/effects.md).

## T2 — Melody, plucks and accents

**Use for:** melodic ambient, electronica, bass hooks, arps. Start with a Reference A4 Note Sequencer, Free Hz oscillator (440 Hz, Semitones tracking) and Pitch amount 1. Enter, for example, D4 E4 F4 A4 G4 E4; choose step duration deliberately. At 80 BPM, quarter-note steps and a quarter-note Clock produce one pitch target per 0.75 s. Clock → AD Envelope Trigger (Trigger / clock mode, amount 1); envelope → oscillator Amplitude (base 0, amount 0.12, Attack 6 ms, Decay 400 ms, curve 3). The clock strikes at boundaries, not on initial startup; the first slot may not strike until wrap. A pitch-only sequencer and clock do not supply a per-note gate. For independent sparse melody hits use T3. For a free accent, try 0.05 Hz Clock (20 s period), 8 ms attack and 1.5 s decay, independent slow pitch targets; choose timings or verify that changes during audible tails are musically acceptable. Use distinct lead/answer register and short echo tails for clarity. To perform a note live, change the pitch and trigger/gate independently, or use a continuous voice until Note-mode articulation is implemented.

Contracts: [sequencer](../../instruments/note-sequencer.md), [envelopes](../../audio/envelopes.md), [timing](../../audio/transport-and-timing.md).

## T3 — Rhythm grid, patterns and rests

**Use for:** dance drums, broken beats, bass syncopation, generative cycles. `seconds per beat = 60/BPM`; a sixteenth is 0.25 beats. A bar in 4/4 has 16 such slots. A quarter Clock fires every beat, a synchronized 1/16 Clock every quarter beat. Use typed Clock → AD Trigger at unity. Single Clock fan-out aligns events; to get independently offset/sparse hits use a separate amplitude mask rather than treating every Clock as an arbitrary pattern. Synchronization starts at transport zero; test startup and wrap rather than assuming a trigger on slot 1 at start.

**Current workaround for editable rests/accents:** Note Sequencer Pitch returns a numeric semitone offset. As an ordinary modulation source, it can intentionally encode a bounded level pattern rather than musical pitch. Example: Reference C4 (MIDI 60), 16 synchronized steps, one quarter-beat per step, Glide 0; enter C4 (offset 0) for a rest, C5 (+12) for full level, F#4 (+6) for half level (not Hold). Connect Pitch to a separate Gain's Gain control with base 0 and amount `1/12` per semitone; effective gain is 0, 1 or 0.5. Place the Gain **after** the envelope-shaped drum/voice and **before** its delay/reverb so gaps do not cut existing effect tails. The Clock still triggers the voice every grid boundary; the Gain mask suppresses unwanted hits. Use separate mask lanes for independent rhythms, share a mask when rhythms agree. When attack + decay exceeds the grid period, even masked triggers retrigger the envelope internally, and a closed mask can truncate a long drum tail. For short masked hits at 140 BPM, start with a total envelope shorter than a sixteenth (~107 ms); for longer kicks/snares, use a slower matching clock or accept truncated/retriggered tails. Sustained bass can use a continuous pitched generator with its own level mask and short glide rather than retriggering AD at every slot. Small control slews and overlapping adjacent steps can affect exact transients; audition/measure boundary behavior. For precise note durations, velocity and rests this remains a workaround, not implemented Note-mode sequencing.

| Pattern (1-based slots at 1/16) | Kick | Snare/clap | Hat/detail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Four-on-floor | 1, 5, 9, 13 | 5, 13 | 3, 7, 11, 15 |
| Half-time bass | 1, 8 | 9 | 3, 7, 11, 15 |
| Broken / 2-step-inspired | 1, 7, 11 | 5, 13 | 3, 6, 10, 15 |
| D&B two-step-inspired | 1, 11 | 5, 13 | 3, 7, 11, 15 (or quieter sixteenths) |

These are starter patterns, not definitive genre rhythms. Use a 32- or 64-step mask for phrase variation, e.g. slightly different end-of-bar fills, and use quiet accents. Assign independent, deliberately chosen odd cycle lengths (e.g. 5 and 7 quarter-beat steps) for phased rhythms, but avoid calling these probability or Euclidean generation. Free clocks do not automatically produce swing or sample-accurate offsets on a common grid; if precise swing or arbitrary event placement is critical, the current workaround may not supply it. A Hold keeps the previous value (including a nonzero mask). For steady clocks without variable rests, omit the mask to save lanes. Reachable Note Sequencers and manual actions share a max-30 lane budget; count them across composites before adding masks or macro controls.

## T4 — Sub and kick foundation

**Use for:** bass music, dance, ambient bass. Sub: sine oscillator at F1 43.65 Hz or G1 49 Hz, semitone tracking, base amplitude 0.12 (starting value; use a separate Gate/AD or gain mask for rests). Pitch changes/glides can make an 808-like bass; a short attack and release-like fade reduce clicks, and intentional sustain distinguishes it from kick. Choose glide relative to note length. Keep sub dry, avoid unnecessary low-band modulation or high Q. Add upper harmonics from a separate quiet triangle/mid-bass lane for playback systems unable to reproduce deep fundamentals.

Kick: free sine oscillator, Frequency base 50–60 Hz, Amplitude base 0. Shared Clock or Manual Trigger to two AD envelopes (typed Trigger / clock): pitch envelope Attack 1 ms, Decay 50–100 ms, decay curve 3, Frequency depth ~100–180 Hz; amp envelope Attack 3 ms, Decay 120–200 ms, curve 3, amplitude depth 0.20 initially. For a sharper kick add a quiet high-passed noise transient (T7); for a long pitched kick shorten neighboring sub events. The editable Impact — AD percussion composite is a starting point, with its internal audio connected to the mirrored Output Rack, not a second Master output. See [envelopes](../../audio/envelopes.md).

Separate kick and bass rhythmically or duck the bass predictably. A grid-matched mask (T3) can leave kick slots empty; an envelope triggered by the same kick clock can drive a separate bass Gain with base 1 and negative amount (e.g. -0.7), fast attack and timed decay, if the clock genuinely only strikes actual kick events. If masked kick events are suppressed after the clock, the same clock still triggers ducking; choose a matching rhythm or accept the musical effect. This is deliberate clock-driven amplitude shaping, not an audio sidechain detector or compressor. Check bass recovery, accumulated energy and mono sum. Apply [mix guidance](../music-theory-and-sound-design.md#6-perceived-loudness-gain-staging-and-dynamic-range).

## T5 — Noise and textural gestures

**Use for:** atmosphere, dark ambient, glitch, risers, industrial. Pink noise → band-pass Filter (base cutoff e.g. 650 Hz, Q 0.8) → Gain 0.04 → Reverb. Slow modulation of the cutoff (0.017 Hz sine, amount 450 Hz) and gain (0.011 Hz sine, amount 0.035) give spectral and amplitude movement without clipping controls at zero. For a wind-like burst use white/pink Noise with base amplitude zero, triggered by AD (e.g. 1500 ms attack, 4500 ms decay, amplitude depth 0.06) into low/high-pass or band-pass. For tiny glitches, use envelope attacks/decays of 1–30 ms, frequency bands separated from other voices, and sparse masks. For granular/tape-like clouds, layer a few independently timed, gently pitched and tapered synth voices with different periods and modulated delay; identify it as a synthesized approximation, not actual grains or recorded loops. Brown noise emphasizes low frequencies; be deliberate with high-pass to avoid masking sub. Do not turn up noise until it sounds exciting; use contrast and space.

For unobtrusive ambience, start with lower noise amplitude and post-effect trim, check whether the dry noise is recognizable at the sparsest part of the arrangement, and transform higher-level noise deliberately rather than relying on reverb alone. See [noise transformation recipes](../sound-quality-and-voice-design.md#2-treat-noise-as-material-not-a-finished-texture).

Contracts: [oscillators and noise](../../audio/oscillators-and-noise.md), [effects](../../audio/effects.md), [envelope modes](../../audio/envelopes.md).

## T6 — Moving mid-bass and metallic tones

**Use for:** experimental bass, neurofunk, acid, techno, industrial. Create distinct calls/responses, not identical stacks of all these sounds.

- **Reese-like:** two saw oscillators at F2 (87.31 Hz) and 87.31 × `2^(9/1200)` Hz, amp 0.07 each, optionally a third lower-level voice; shared pitch pattern; sum → high-pass ~120 Hz → low-pass base 850 Hz, Q 1.1, modulated by unipolar AD depth 1400 Hz or slow bipolar LFO depth 600 Hz → shaped gain → mixer. Detuning creates beating; the separate dry sub should not undergo the same modulation. Mono chorus can add motion above sub.
- **Resonant/acid-like:** square or saw at A2, pitch steps and glide, amplitude AD 4/120 ms (amount 0.10), low-pass cutoff base 350 Hz, Q 2–4 to start, second AD (1/150 ms) with depth ~1500 Hz on cutoff. Modulate accents/cutoff with a slow lane; avoid saturating at bounds. A low-pass alone is not a TB-303 circuit model.
- **Growl-like:** use harmonically rich saw/square, band-pass moving from ~400 to 1500 Hz with a rhythmic AD or LFO, Q ~2, local gain envelope. Add another filtered copy in a different range at lower level for changing formant-like impression. This is not a dedicated vowel/formant filter or wavetable growl. Try changing one cutoff trajectory per response rather than sweeping every voice simultaneously.
- **Audio-rate frequency modulation:** use a free sine modulator at 150 Hz (amp 1) → carrier free Frequency (base 300 Hz, amount ±100 Hz via positive depth 100 and bipolar signal); carrier amp base 0, AD depth 0.07. Adjust rates and depth intentionally; keep base−depth positive and within actual frequency range. Change modulator frequency ratios for pitched/inharmonic color. This is additive linear-Hz modulation of frequency, not a dedicated phase-modulation/FM operator bank; semitone tracking and modulation order can matter, so verify behavior for changing pitch. Use high-pass if the resulting voice competes with sub. Avoid unnecessary high modulation frequencies and check spectrum for unwanted artifacts.
- **Tremolo/chops:** bipolar LFO into amplitude or gain with nonzero base and matching positive amount (e.g. 0.05 ±0.05). At control rates it makes pulse/groove; at audio rates it introduces sidebands. Nonnegative clamping makes this different from four-quadrant ring modulation; avoid calling it true ring modulation. For exact gaps and accents use T3.
- **Breathing harmonic interaction:** the [listener-validated reference drone](../foundations/breathing-harmonic-drone.md) sends weighted, slightly non-octave saws into both a dry path and a slow sine's clamped amplitude input, recombines them, and coordinates brightness with that shaping cycle. Use the underlying interaction principle for evolving dark pads, industrial textures or bass gestures; inspect the original's large peaks and subsonic energy before adapting it. Explore other relationships instead of copying the same voice into every track.

Mix voices into independent local Gains; automated or manual arrangement moves them in/out. Current graph cannot be assumed to offer distortion, resampling, wavetable scanning, multiband dynamics, true stereo or authentic nonlinear bass processing. See [effects](../../audio/effects.md), [oscillators](../../audio/oscillators-and-noise.md), [modulation](../../core/parameters-and-modulation.md). Use the creative differences between these simple voices instead of simulating a complex sound-design chain with undocumented nodes.

## T7 — Percussion and impact

Common source: Clock → typed AD, Envelope output → generator amplitude with zero base. Combine independent envelopes for body/noise, use gain masks T3 for rhythm. Example starting settings (milliseconds and amplitudes):

| Sound | Sources and envelope | Filtering / role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kick | T4 sine frequency sweep and amp envelope | Dry and defined; keep sub space |
| Snare-like | Triangle at ~180 Hz (AD 2/90 ms, 0.08); white noise (AD 2/140 ms, 0.10) | Band-pass noise ~1800 Hz Q 1; body adds pitch |
| Closed hat | White noise, AD 1/35 ms, 0.035 | High-pass ~6500 Hz; crisp quiet timekeeping |
| Open hat | White noise, AD 2/170 ms, 0.035 | High-pass ~4500 Hz; open the offbeats |
| Clap-like | Two noise bands, AD 3/100 ms, 0.06 summed starting amplitude | Band-pass 900 and 2000 Hz; short reverb; not true multi-burst clap |
| Tom/log-drum-like | Sine or triangle base 110 Hz, pitch AD 1/70 ms, +80 Hz; amp AD 2/250 ms, 0.12 | Tune body to key if used melodically; keep space for kick |
| Bell/metallic impact | Two quieter sine partials at deliberately noninteger ratio, AD 3/600 ms and 1/260 ms | Dissimilar decays, short echo or reverb |

For a shaker, use a soft noise AD and alternating mask levels. For a break-inspired rhythm, build kick/snare/ghost-hat lanes and accents on a 16/32-step grid; this is an original synthesized beat, not a chopped break recording. For transitions use pitched or noise swells, short fill variations and silence. Refine by hearing how the mix feels, not just checking exact envelopes. ## T8 — Delay, reverb and depth

Use 2–3 perceived depth planes: dry/direct foundation (kick/sub and selected motif), middle (shorter delay/reverb), distant (longer damped tails on pads/accents). Example dub stab: short AD chord → low-pass → synchronized dotted-quarter Delay (feedback 0.40, wet 0.35) → Reverb (decay 3 s, damping 0.6, wet 0.15) → arrangement gain. The echo spacing is 1.5 beats; choose relation to rhythm. For an ambient distant accent, use 6–10 s decay, damping ~0.6, wet 0.5 and modest input level. The maximum currently documented reverb decay is 12 s; delay free time is bounded to 0.01–2 s (synchronized uses the BPM division); feedback ≤0.9. Avoid placing long wet reverb on the entire bass/drum mix. Damping and high-pass/light filtering help separate depth planes. Current instances are mono; sound may feel layered/deep, not true stereo panned or immersive. Do not create editable graph feedback cycles; use the internal supported feedback of Delay. See [effects](../../audio/effects.md) and [theory: depth](../music-theory-and-sound-design.md#8-depth-clarity-and-the-illusion-of-space).

## T9 — Long-form arrangement

Design the piece first: roles, tonal/harmonic anchors, sections, transitions and end. Put each voice's final Audio into its composite Output Rack (if grouped) and use parent Gains to feed the root Mixer → conservative master Gain → Master output. Expose useful controls and give voices meaningful names where supported; use an accompanying performance note until [patch annotations](../../editor/patch-annotations.md) are available. The editable composite's rack mirrors ports; no internal Master output is needed. For a live performance, set root gains at appropriate levels and perform section changes without rebuilding topology. End with a planned fade or stop sending new events while allowing effects to decay.

**Optional finite-horizon macro workaround** (current looping pitch sequencer, not a scene sequencer): use the T3 numeric level encoding with longer free steps and glide, to control each layer's arrangement Gain. For a 10-minute fixed-tempo/free-time piece choose 30 steps × 20 s = 600 s loop horizon; Reference C4, explicit C4–C5 targets with depths 1/12 into Gain base 0, glide e.g. 12 s. Set Initial pitch to match the intended start (often 0), design the first slot intentionally; entries with C4 are explicit zero. Example for a melody: slots 1–4 C4, 5–8 F#4, 9–13 A4, 14–16 E4, 17–19 C4, 20–24 C5, 25–27 G4, 28 E4, 29 C4, 30 C4. The melody reaches zero after the fade toward slot 29, leaving time for delay/reverb if that fade is before the effects. This is a coarse continuous level envelope made from pitch values; no automatic Stop, built-in timeline, arbitrary curve/per-step duration or individual note gate is implied. The pattern wraps at 600 s; stop after the intended duration or arrange an intentionally repeating performance. To preserve tails, fade excitation before effects while allowing final post-effect faders to remain audible, then lower master for final silence as appropriate. A 10-minute piece with a clean ending must account for longest tail and the audible loop boundary.

Share arrangement lanes among compatible groups to conserve reachable lane count, count pitch patterns/level masks/macro lanes together against current 30-lane limit, and keep slow modulation clocks independent from tempo lanes where musically useful. Prefer manual arrangement when macro workaround is awkward or compromises the intended music. For a bar-based composition, synchronize macro steps to appropriate beat lengths within supported ranges; calculate duration at the chosen BPM and verify resets. Do not assign a 64-step sequencer an unverified multi-minute synchronized division. See [composition guide](../generative-ambient-composition.md#4-how-to-keep-an-hour-interesting), [sequencer](../../instruments/note-sequencer.md), [composites](../../editor/composite-nodes.md), [reusable modules plan](../../editor/reusable-modules.md), [roadmap](../../../ROADMAP.md).

## Capability and verification boundary

| Musical need | Present approach | Gap / future direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Note articulation and precise rhythms | Pitch + Clock/AD, separate encoded level masks; manual Trigger/Gate | [Note-mode gate, trigger, duration and velocity design](../../instruments/note-sequencer-design.md), richer event patterns |
| Long-form variation | Independent cycles, manual faders, finite loop macro masks | Scene/arrangement sequencer, probability, explicit automation curves |
| Sample-based genres and granular work | Original synthesized drum/texture approximations | [Sample assets](../../audio/sample-assets.md), granular/slicing processing |
| Organic and complex sound design | Filter, EQ, delay, chorus, reverb, oscillators, noise, audio-rate modulation | [Physical modelling](../../instruments/physical-modelling.md), dedicated nonlinear, wavetable and advanced processing as needed |
| Mixing and spatial design | Separate bands/levels, controllable envelopes, depth planes | Stereo routing, dynamics and broader mixing tools |

These gaps are musical observations, not implementation promises or new assigned milestones. Check the current [roadmap](../../../ROADMAP.md) and source; propose improvements separately when user scope warrants. Verify a patch's structural validity, graph output, control timing, peak/headroom (aim to leave several dB margin; don't assume Gain protects against all internal peaks), intended rests, pitch, mix clarity and contrast. If exporting, ensure graph-owned IDs, racks and required parameters conform to [persistence](../../editor/persistence-and-history.md). Render/measure where possible and listen for musical qualities; report exactly what was verified and whether the intended duration requires user action. A technical workaround that passes graph validation may still perform poorly musically. Choose a simpler arrangement rather than hiding limitations.
