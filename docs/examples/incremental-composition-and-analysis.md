# Incremental AI composition and audio analysis

[Music creation](README.md) · [Production workflow](production-workflow.md) · [Acceptance gates](musical-composition-validation.md) · [Agent Kit](../core/agent-kit.md) · [Phase 7.8](../roadmap/advanced.md#phase-78--incremental-composition-and-analysis-planned)

## Purpose and evidence

Owner-approved direction: generate and evaluate music in bounded, meaningful increments rather than build an entire complex graph before looking for problems. This is a proposed methodology and tooling plan, not experimentally established quality improvement. Apply the workflow now with available evidence; automated analyzers and native headless execution remain planned. Preserve the existing musical acceptance gates and the patch-only Agent Kit contract.

Incremental work reduces the search space, keeps successful material reusable and makes faults easier to localize. It can also optimize sounds for solo listening at the expense of the mix, prematurely lock artistic decisions, or waste more compute and agent context than whole-piece generation. Treat each component as a provisional decision, not an irrevocable approval. Musical relationships and an early representative combined passage matter more than making every instrument individually impressive. Do not prescribe a universal order of instruments, target spectral shape, harmony, or number of layers across genres.

## Staged generation workflow

1. **Discovery and non-generative planning.** Read routed music/genre/production guidance, listener-rated foundations and version-matched native catalog; check intended role, duration, form, energy trajectory, constraints and acceptance criteria. Plan motifs, harmonic/rhythmic material, role-by-section matrix and register/space allocation before constructing patches. Identify missing native capabilities. Choose a bounded iteration budget and representative audition fragments before generation.
2. **Individual voices and layers.** Generate a small native patch or a named subgraph with a realistic role-specific driver. Validate structure and inspect the musical event data first. When native samples exist, measure the relevant features and compare with the intended role and evidence-labeled references. For example, a kick can be examined for attack, pitch trajectory, body and decay, a drone for long-term modulation and spectral evolution. Do not add a layer solely to increase spectral richness. Keep a reproducible snapshot and rationale when the component satisfies its available checks; record unavailable evidence explicitly.
3. **Early contextual integration.** Combine the central musical relationships (kick and bass, lead and accompaniment, interacting drones, etc.) in a representative passage before finishing every sound. Check timing, articulation, register, masking, relative levels, harmonic/rhythmic interplay, and whether the intended focal role survives. Shared processing can change the result; solo output is not proof of in-context contribution. Revise earlier choices when necessary.
4. **Sections and transitions.** Develop and validate sections with explicit roles, evolving material, contrast, intentional gaps and transitions. Preserve independently successful phrases and sound choices; use incremental edits rather than regenerate the entire patch for an isolated fault. Do not reset oscillator/modulator phase, delay/reverb tails, transport or random state when a section is intended to represent continuous playback.
5. **Whole-piece integration.** Combine sections into the native deliverable. Recheck graph, score, section roles, transitions, ending and duration; where samples are available evaluate whole-mix headroom, dynamics, balance, and long-duration behavior. Listen or obtain listener feedback separately; objective metrics do not establish musical quality. Follow the mandatory whole-piece gates.
6. **Targeted iteration and stop.** Record the concrete problem, likely source, bounded change, and before/after evidence under equivalent conditions. Recheck changed components and dependent groups/sections, then the combined piece. Keep the prior accepted candidate if evidence does not justify the change. Stop at the agreed budget or acceptance criteria; disclose unresolved findings rather than chasing arbitrary metric targets.

### Progressive acceptance and evidence

Track a component's stable graph/role identity, snapshot or hash, test fixture, expected behavior, evidence type, observations, dependencies, revision, and next action. Use Draft, individually checked, contextually checked, whole-piece checked, or needs revision. Each state must separately identify structural, acoustic and musical/listener evidence as pass, fail, partial, unavailable or not applicable. A patch-only structural check can never implicitly pass acoustic or listener gates. Invalidate affected checks when parameters, routing, score, shared effects, runtime revision or neighboring layers materially change; preserve unaffected evidence. Mark user-confirmed preferences separately from agent hypotheses.

Use proportional checks: single kicks often need 0.5–2 s, percussion patterns 4–8 s, bass/melodic phrases 8–16 s, evolving drones 20–60 s, groups 15–30 s, sections their complete span, and the final piece its intended duration. These are starting heuristics, not limits. Include enough warm-up, transport history and decay tails; use longer windows for slow modulation or feedback. When history cannot be reproduced, label a fragment a standalone audition rather than an exact excerpt of the whole composition. Choose metric resolution and analysis duration by the question, not every possible analyzer for every sound.

## Analysis without and with audio

### Structural analysis (no DSP required)

The existing patch-only kit is intentionally independent of browser, Faust compilation, audio rendering and editor runtime. An optional structural analyzer can consume native JSON and generated catalog metadata to expose score/sequence event counts and timing, rests, duration and articulation variation, repeated patterns, pitch/register and harmonic relationships, role activity by section, routing/reachability, parameter/envelope configuration and modulation relationships. Account for native timing semantics, conditional ports, nested graph ownership and unsupported behavior. Static data cannot determine actual audibility, effective time-dependent pitch, sound quality or processed waveform; label unknowns and separate musical advisories from native validation failures. Prefer deterministic, compact machine-readable results with source graph paths, time/section scope, units and limitations. Avoid genre-specific universal thresholds and duplicate validators.

### Acoustic analysis (actual samples required)

Without DSP execution or a real captured/recorded audio input there is no spectrogram, waveform, true loudness or phase measurement of a generated patch. A predicted plot from parameter values is not evidence. Audio file analysis may be implemented and calibrated independently; for agent-created patches, native playback capture or a separately optional headless native DSP executor is required. Executing native DSP into numeric buffers is technically rendering even if no browser, speaker, MP3 or persistent WAV is involved.

Existing [signal observation](../audio/signal-observation.md) documents passive native taps, bounded FFT and offline Faust/WASM probes. Audit actual source, dependencies, scheduling, supported node types, compile/runtime identity, deterministic seeds, state continuity, sample rates and effect tails before promising arbitrary patch execution. Prove native parity on controlled fixtures and measure compute/memory/startup cost. Do not reimplement a competing synthesizer in Python, make rendering a Phase 7.7 release dependency, or claim that test probes already constitute a general renderer. If parity or cost is unacceptable, stop the executor effort and support captured samples or structural-only workflows instead. Prefer bounded in-memory samples and optional export, not mandatory audio files or UI.

| Tool | Useful for | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waveform, sample peak, RMS, crest factor | Clipping, envelope, attack/decay, level trends | Not perceived loudness or guaranteed true peak |
| FFT and band energy | Tonal balance, narrow resonances, harmonic/body content | Time averaging can conceal attacks; overlaps suggest but do not prove masking |
| Spectrogram (STFT) | Time-frequency structure, transients, pitch sweep, harmonic evolution, noise and texture | Window/hop trade-off; no intrinsic quality or preference score |
| Onset/pitch/chroma analysis | Rhythmic variation and tonal evidence on suitable material | Polyphony, noise, percussion, pitch bends and unpitched drones limit confidence |
| Calibrated loudness/true peak | Mix and delivery comparison | Requires appropriate standard and comparable durations |
| Vectorscope, correlation, mid/side energy | Actual L/R balance, stereo spread, mono compatibility | Requires synchronized genuine stereo channels; not meaningful for the documented current mono master |

For example, a short kick audition may show a weak broadband attack or excessively long sub decay relative to the intended groove. A targeted edit to a native layer, envelope or routing may help, but added noise or harmonic energy is not inherently better. A drone may require substantially longer windows to establish evolution. A combined section with several individually rich voices may mask the lead; analyze role-specific taps and the actual mix, not just independent solo patches. Nonlinear shared buses mean stem summation or muting is not necessarily equivalent to the original output. Distinguish pre/post processing and document tap position; preserve the audible graph and never mix observation channels into output.

## Agent-facing evidence and experimental guardrails

Prefer numerical summaries and targeted plots to large raw arrays and indiscriminate images. Reports should include native build/catalog/patch identity, analysis source and evidence type, sample rate/channel layout if applicable, time ranges, signal/graph addresses, calibration/window settings, metrics with units, confidence/availability, and actionable observations clearly separated from measurements. Save candidate differences and accepted checkpoints; reuse only unchanged compatible evidence. No automatic changes purely to maximize a metric. Human/listener evaluation remains separate.

Use existing listener-rated reference patches where actual evidence exists; obtain consent/provenance for audio assets and never invent ratings or measured signatures. Compare similar intended roles under matched conditions. Treat reference profiles as examples, not mandatory spectral templates.

Evaluate the staged approach empirically: compare whole-piece baseline versus incremental structural checks first, then optionally the same workflow with acoustic fragments. Use matching briefs, native capabilities and comparable agent/compute budgets; use blind or randomized listener comparisons where practical, log failure categories, patch validity, time, token use, execution cost, iterations and listener preferences. Distinguish technical improvement from musical creativity. No numeric quality gain is currently demonstrated. Proceed to more expensive tooling only if incremental evidence shows value. Vectorscope/stereo analysis is a later dependency on actual stereo routing (Phase 7.5); no need to delay mono analysis.
